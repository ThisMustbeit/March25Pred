    Why give patients a taper calendar?

    1 / 10

    Tapers and medication incidents

    Tapers are a common source of medication incidents

    ISMP Canada analysed 122 tapering and titration incidents reported by Canadian community pharmacies (2010–2014); the majority involved corticosteroids. It recommends giving patients a tapering schedule tool, such as a personalized calendar or booklet.

    1. Chen A, Liang S, Ho C. Medication incidents involving drug tapering in community pharmacy: a multi-incident analysis by ISMP Canada. Pharmacy Connection. Fall 2014:34–37.

    Prednisone

    Put the stop plan in writing

    “It is important to provide clear written instructions to the patient about when and how to stop prednisone, including detailed instructions for dose tapering if required.” Medsafe New Zealand, after a patient reported steroid withdrawal symptoms.

    1. Medsafe. Prednisone treatment – follow dosing recommendations. Prescriber Update. 2021;42(2):21–22.

    Glucocorticoid guideline

    Guidelines call for patient education

    The 2024 European Society of Endocrinology and Endocrine Society guideline recommends that clinicians who start glucocorticoids educate patients, and lists withdrawal symptoms as a topic to discuss with patients on long-term therapy when tapering begins.

    1. Beuschlein F, et al. European Society of Endocrinology and Endocrine Society joint clinical guideline: diagnosis and therapy of glucocorticoid-induced adrenal insufficiency. Eur J Endocrinol. 2024;190(5):G25–G51.

    Label comprehension

    Standard labels are often misread

    When 395 primary care patients explained how they would take five labelled medications, about 1 in 5 instructions were misunderstood. Errors were more likely with lower health literacy and with more medications.

    1. Davis TC, et al. Literacy and misunderstanding prescription drug labels. Ann Intern Med. 2006;145(12):887–894.

    Complex regimens

    Patients struggle to organize complex regimens

    When 464 primary care patients were asked how they would schedule a typical seven-drug regimen, many planned more dosing times than needed and missed chances to take compatible drugs together. Low health literacy was the strongest predictor.

    1. Wolf MS, Curtis LM, Waite K, et al. Helping patients simplify and safely use complex prescription regimens. Arch Intern Med. 2011;171(4):300–305.

    Day-by-day reminders

    Showing when each dose is due helps

    A Cochrane review of 12 randomized trials (2,196 people) looked at packaging that shows the date or time each dose is due. In the 4 trials that counted pills, it increased the percentage of pills taken by 11 points on average, though results varied widely.

    1. Mahtani KR, Heneghan CJ, Glasziou PP, Perera R. Reminder packaging for improving adherence to self-administered long-term medications. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2011;(9):CD005025.

    Beyond steroids

    A written taper plan helps patients act

    In the EMPOWER trial in Montreal pharmacies (303 older adults), a booklet that included a tapering protocol led 27% to stop their benzodiazepine within 6 months, compared with 4.5% with usual care.

    1. Tannenbaum C, et al. Reduction of inappropriate benzodiazepine prescriptions among older adults through direct patient education: the EMPOWER cluster randomized trial. JAMA Intern Med. 2014;174(6):890–898.

    Illustrated schedules

    Visual schedules help most when regimens are complex

    In a randomized trial of 435 patients with heart disease, illustrated daily schedules did not improve adherence overall. In a post-hoc analysis, patients taking more than eight medications had about twice the odds of adherence with them. In a 20-person pilot with older adults, self-efficacy and adherence increased.

    1. Kripalani S, Schmotzer B, Jacobson TA. Improving medication adherence through graphically enhanced interventions in coronary heart disease (IMAGE-CHD): a randomized controlled trial. J Gen Intern Med. 2012;27(12):1609–1617.
    2. Martin D, Kripalani S, et al. Improving medication management among at-risk older adults. J Gerontol Nurs. 2012;38(6):24–37.

    Written plans with steroids

    Individualized written plans improve outcomes

    In a review of 26 randomized trials, individualized written asthma action plans that said when and how to step up treatment, including starting oral corticosteroids, consistently improved outcomes.

    1. Gibson PG, Powell H. Written action plans for asthma: an evidence-based review of the key components. Thorax. 2004;59(2):94–99.

    In practice

    Hand over the calendar, then talk it through

    ISMP Canada advises that a tapering calendar be given together with face-to-face counselling and appropriate follow-up, not on its own.

    1. Chen A, Liang S, Ho C. Medication incidents involving drug tapering in community pharmacy: a multi-incident analysis by ISMP Canada. Pharmacy Connection. Fall 2014:34–37.
